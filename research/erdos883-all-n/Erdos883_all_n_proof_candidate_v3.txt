Erdős 883 exact all n proof candidate
Version 3 4 October 2026 UTC

Abstract
We give a proof candidate for the first question of Erdős 883 for every
finite n. The proof uses odd totient-profile smoothing, small-prime
signature ordering and an ordered-Hall construction. An explicit analytic
argument handles n>=2,000,000. Exact interval certificates cover every
smaller n, for every permitted subset A. No regularity lemma or unspecified
starting threshold remains in the proposed argument.

Theorem
For every integer n>=0 and every A subset {1,...,n} satisfying
 |A|>floor(n/2)+floor(n/3)-floor(n/6),
the graph on A with distinct vertices u,v adjacent exactly when gcd(u,v)=1
contains a simple cycle of every odd length L with 3<=L<=n/3+1.
Equivalently, it contains C_(2ell+1) for every integer 1<=ell<=floor(n/6).

Status and attribution
This is an AI-assisted proof candidate whose mathematical arguments and
exact certificates have received independent AI-assisted adversarial
checks. It has not received external peer review or Lean/formal verification.
No first-solution, novelty or priority claim is made.
The proof explicitly adapts Donald Della Pietra's earlier missing-even
smoothing, totient profiles, prime signatures and ordered-Hall method.
His pinned 27 July 2026 manuscript states a sufficiently-large-n theorem.
The claimed addition here is the exact finite completion and explicit
threshold. The separate complete-tripartite question is not a new claim.

Organization
Part I proves an exact interval criterion covering all subsets A.
Part II provides adaptive arithmetic bounds and a profile-only certificate.
Part III proves the exact finite-prefix totient estimates.
Part IV proves the theorem for every n>=2,000,000.
Part V documents the complete exact finite bridge.
Section numbers mentioned within a part are local to that part.
All decimal constants in proof inequalities denote exact terminating
rationals. The two decimal expansions of rational error bounds in Part III
are explicitly diagnostic approximations; their exact rational bounds are
authoritative. Displayed decimal approximations in checker output are diagnostic;
no floating-point comparison determines a certificate pass.

The only external theorem
Ahlswede–Khachatrian (1996), Theorem 2(i), specialized to odd integers,
bounds an intersecting odd family by floor(n/3)-floor(n/6). We use it only
for n>=6 and a retained set of at least two odd vertices. If that set
contains 1, it already has a coprime pair; otherwise absence of a coprime
pair makes it intersecting under their convention even when equal pairs
are included. This avoids any singleton-1 convention at small n.
Primary paper: https://matwbn.icm.edu.pl/ksiazki/aa/aa74/aa7424.pdf
Theorem 2(i) is on printed page 149.

PART I Exact finite interval criterion
============================================================

Notation and external input
Let f(n)=floor(n/2)+floor(n/3)-floor(n/6), m(n)=floor(n/6), H(n)=ceil(n/2), and M(n)=floor(n/3)-floor(n/6)+1. Let O_U be the odd integers in [U], in any fixed total order (the implementation uses decreasing phi(v)/v, breaking ties by increasing v). Write P_k for its first k members.

The sole non-elementary arithmetic input is Ahlswede–Khachatrian (1996), Theorem 2, specialized to s=2: a set of pairwise noncoprime odd integers in [n] has size at most floor(n/3)-floor(n/6). Thus any M(n) distinct odds contain two coprime elements.
Primary source:
https://matwbn.icm.edu.pl/ksiazki/aa/aa74/aa7424.pdf
Bibliographic identity: Sets of integers and quasi-integers with pairwise common divisor, Acta Arithmetica 74 (1996), 141–153, DOI 10.4064/aa-74-2-141-153.

Choose any ordered finite list of odd primes p_1,...,p_t. The s-signature of v is (1_{p_i|v})_{1<=i<=s}. Set h_0=0 and h_s=2^s+1 for s>=1.
For integers 6<=L<=U, define:
 e_s(k;L,U) = min over distinct u,v in P_k with equal s-signature of #{even w<=L:gcd(w,u)=gcd(w,v)=1};
 r_s(k;L,U) = the same minimum with all positive w<=L.
If there is no such pair, set the corresponding minimum to +infinity. Finite sentinels exceeding every tested threshold may be used; the implementation uses floor(U/2)+1 and U+1.

Exact interval criterion
Suppose for every integer
 0<=b<=H(U)-M(U), 1<=j<=m(U),
there exist an s in {0,...,t} and k in {0,...,H(U)} such that
 2 max(0,H(U)-k-b)+h_s < j,                     (1)
and at least one of
 e_s(k;L,U)>=b+j,                               (2E)
 r_s(k;L,U)>=U-f(U)+m(U)+j.                     (2R)
Then for every integer n in [L,U] and every A subset [n] with |A|>f(n), G(A) contains every odd cycle C_(2ell+1), 1<=ell<=floor(n/6).

Proof
Fix such n,A and put b=#(even integers in [n] outside A). There are at least M(n)+b selected odds. Retain the first M(n) of these in the fixed O_U order, calling the resulting set Y0. For every k,
 |Y0 outside P_k| <= max(0,H(U)-k-b).             (3)
Indeed, if a retained odd lies after position k, every discarded selected odd lies after k as well; there are at least b discarded odds. Counting inside O_U proves (3). If no retained odd lies after k the assertion is immediate.

By the stated Ahlswede–Khachatrian theorem, choose coprime distinct a,a' in Y0. Since M(n)>=m(n)+1, for each ell<=m(n) choose a skeleton of ell+1 members of Y0 containing a,a'. Sort this skeleton lexicographically by its full t-signatures. Move a to the first position and a' to the last, without changing the relative order of the other vertices. Denote the result o_0=a,...,o_ell=a'.

This one order has, simultaneously for every s>=1, at most h_s gaps joining different s-signatures: before endpoint moves, every prefix-signature occupies one block and there are at most 2^s-1 transitions; deleting a vertex cannot increase the number of transitions (the 0/1 inequality metric obeys the triangle inequality), and inserting each prescribed endpoint at an end increases it by at most one. For s=0 there are no transitions. At most twice the number in (3) of the gaps touch an odd outside P_k.

Let R=A minus the skeleton and let D_i={w in R:gcd(w,o_{i-1})=gcd(w,o_i)=1}. For each rank j<=ell take s,k from the criterion. Every gap except at most 2max(0,H(U)-k-b)+h_s has both endpoints in P_k and equal s-signature.
If (2E) holds, that gap has at least b+j common even neighbors in [L]. At most b are missing from A, and no even vertex is in the odd skeleton, so |D_i|>=j.
If (2R) holds, it has at least U-f(U)+m(U)+j common neighbors in [L]. Removing vertices outside A and in the skeleton costs at most
 (n-|A|)+(ell+1) <= n-f(n)+ell <= U-f(U)+m(U),
so again |D_i|>=j. The last inequality follows from monotonicity of n-f(n)+m(n); writing n=6q+r, it equals 3q+(0,1,1,1,1,2)_r.
Also b<=H(n)-M(n)<=H(U)-M(U), since H(n)-M(n)=2q+(-1,0,0,0,0,1)_r is nondecreasing.

Consequently fewer than j lists D_i have size less than j. Sort the ell lists by increasing cardinality. The jth list has size at least j, so greedy selection supplies distinct representatives w_i. They all lie outside the skeleton. Hence
 o_0,w_1,o_1,...,w_ell,o_ell,o_0
is a simple coprime cycle of length 2ell+1, closing via gcd(a,a')=1. Different ranks may use different s,k or (2E)/(2R), because each bounds the same actual resource lists D_i. This proves the criterion.

Why the implementation checks only one k for each s,b,j
If h_s>=j there is no feasible k. Otherwise the smallest feasible k is
 k=H(U)-b-floor((j-h_s-1)/2).
Both common-neighbor minima are nonincreasing in k, so this k is optimal for satisfying (2E)/(2R). In the ranges tested k>=2. All sorting uses exact rational phi(v)/v; all common-neighbor counts use exact integer gcd and bitsets; there is no floating-point arithmetic in the certificate conditions.

PART II Adaptive arithmetic and efficient certificates
============================================================

Notation
rho(v)=phi(v)/v. Throughout this part, endpoint pairs u,v are odd integers.
The first s odd primes are p_1=3,...,p_s; p=p_(s+1).
For s=0, set p_0=2. The 0-signature is empty, p=p_1=3, and the tail
primes r>p_0 are exactly the odd primes; all endpoint pairs remain odd.
Let U be an integer upper bound on every skeleton vertex and let L<=U.
Let H=ceil(U/2), q_L=floor(L/2), m=floor(U/6),
f=floor(U/2)+floor(U/3)-floor(U/6), M=f-floor(U/2)+1.
For 0<z<=1 define the strict finite profile count
 R_U(z)=#{odd v<=U:rho(v)<z}.
Strict inequality ensures that every remaining vertex has profile >=z.
Write h_0=0 and h_s=2^s+1 for s>=1.

1. Exact adaptive tail factor
Let a be the greatest nonnegative integer with p^a<=U and put
 eta_s(U)=((p-1)/p)^a.
Every v<=U has at most a distinct prime divisors exceeding p_s. Each
corresponding totient factor is at least (p-1)/p. Therefore
 product_{r|v, r>p_s prime}(1-1/r) >= eta_s(U).
All ingredients are integers or rational numbers; logarithms are unnecessary
for computing a.

If odd integers u,v<=U share their first s prime-divisibility signatures, every prime
factor of v absent from u exceeds p_s. Hence
 rho(lcm(u,v))
  =rho(u) product_{r|v, r not dividing u}(1-1/r)
  >=rho(u) eta_s(U).
Interchanging u,v proves the slightly stronger bound
 rho(lcm(u,v))>=max(rho(u),rho(v)) eta_s(U).
In particular it is >=z eta_s(U) when both profiles are >=z.

One may sharpen eta_s(U) without changing the proof. Let r be maximal such
that the product of the first r primes after p_s is <=U. No integer <=U
has more than r distinct tail prime factors. Among any r such primes the
smallest r give the smallest totient-factor product. Thus their factor
product is a valid sharper rational eta_s(U).

2. Exact common-neighbor discrepancy
For odd squarefree D with w=omega(D)>=1 and integer X>=0,
 C_D(X)=#{1<=a<=X:gcd(a,D)=1}
       =sum_{d|D} mu(d) floor(X/d).
Comparing with X phi(D)/D, only the positive-mu terms can contribute
negatively. There are 2^(w-1) such divisors, and d=1 has zero fractional
part. Thus
 C_D(X)>=X rho(D)-(2^(w-1)-1).
For D=1 the equality C_1(X)=X holds.

Define W(U) as the greatest w such that the product of the first w odd
primes is <=U^2, and Delta(U)=2^(W(U)-1)-1 (take zero if W(U)=0).
For D=rad(uv), D<=uv<=U^2, so the previous inequality gives
 common even neighbors in [L] >= q_L rho(D)-Delta(U),
 common whole-pool neighbors in [L] >= L rho(D)-Delta(U).
This does not require D to divide L and remains valid if an endpoint is 1.

3. Exact finite criterion using only a univariate profile
For every required pair 0<=b<=H-M and 1<=j<=m, suppose one can choose a
signature prefix s and rational z with
 2 max(0,R_U(z)-b)+h_s<j,                               (A)
and either
 q_L z eta_s(U)-Delta(U)>=b+j,                         (B_E)
or
 L z eta_s(U)-Delta(U)>=U-f+m+j.                       (B_R)
Then the all-subsets conclusion of the finite interval criterion holds for
every n in [L,U].

Proof: retain the best M(n) selected odds as before. Among the R_U(z) low
profile odds, at most max(0,R_U(z)-b) can remain in that retained set,
because at least b selected odds worse than any retained low-profile odd
were discarded. Order the skeleton by a single sufficiently long prime
signature and move its coprime endpoints to the ends. For any selected
prefix s, all but the number in (A) of its gaps have equal signatures and
both endpoint profiles >=z. Sections 1 and 2 give at least j resources
for each such gap. The ordered-Hall proof applies unchanged.

Different ranks may select different prefix lengths. A single ordering by
the longest selected prefix has all shorter prefixes contiguous before
moving endpoints, so there is no consistency problem.

4. One fully uniform tail bound with only O(sqrt U) transitions
For every integer U>1024 choose the least s with U<=4^s. Thus s>=6 and
2^s<2 sqrt U (or the weaker <= suffices).
A valid tail factor for every v<=U is at least 9/10. Consequently
 h_s<=2 sqrt U+1,
 rho(lcm(u,v))>=(9/10) max(rho(u),rho(v))
whenever the two signatures agree.

Proof for s>=511: the next odd prime p>=2s+3>1024. If a is the number
of distinct tail primes in v, then p^a<=U<=4^s, giving
 a/p <= s log4/(p log p)
      < log2/log(2s+3) <1/10.
Bernoulli's inequality gives (1-1/p)^a>=1-a/p>9/10.
For 6<=s<=510, exact rational arithmetic checks the sharper tail factor
from Section 1 with upper bound 4^s. Every case is >=9/10. The smallest
factor is 396/437 at s=6, from the two tail primes 19 and 23. This finite
parameter calculation is included in verify_adaptive_tail_lemma.py; it
is not enumeration over n or over subsets A. The standalone verifier reported about 0.04 CPU seconds.

A useful uniform discrepancy bound is also elementary. For odd squarefree D,
 (2^omega(D))^4/D=product_{p|D}(16/p)
 <=16^5/(3*5*7*11*13)<81,
since factors from primes >=17 are <=1. Hence 2^omega(D)<3 D^(1/4).
For D<=U^2, the actual discrepancy from Section 2 is strictly less than
(3/2)sqrt U. Thus (B_E)/(B_R) may use loss (3/2)sqrt U and factor 9/10,
without computing W(U).

This proves a universal numerical reduction for all U>1024:
 2 max(0,R_U(z)-b)+2^s+1<j,
and either
 (9/10)q_L z-(3/2)sqrt U>=b+j,
or
 (9/10)L z-(3/2)sqrt U>=U-f+m+j
are sufficient. The exact integer 2^s+1 is preferable to its displayed
2 sqrt U+1 upper bound. Irrational square roots may be rounded upward
in the subtracted error if a rational-only certificate is desired.

7. Stronger density bound and independent audit of the fast finite test
Always
 rho(lcm(u,v))=rho(u)rho(v)/rho(gcd(u,v))>=rho(u)rho(v).
Consequently, for both profiles >=z and equal s-signatures, the valid lower
bound is
 g_s(z)=max(z^2,eta_s(U)z).
This may replace z eta_s(U) everywhere in Section 3, and substantially
improves shorter prefixes. In particular, the z^2 term needs no signatures
at all and can be used at s=0, where h_0=0.

For each odd v<=U define integer lower-degree functions
 d_E(v)=floor(q_L g_s(rho(v)))-Delta(U),
 d_R(v)=floor(L g_s(rho(v)))-Delta(U).
Both are nondecreasing functions of rho(v). If two endpoints each have
d_E(v)>=K and share their s-signatures, their actual common-even-neighbor
count is >=K. The analogous assertion holds for d_R. Thus define
 R_E(K)=#{odd v<=U:d_E(v)<K},
 R_R(K)=#{odd v<=U:d_R(v)<K}.
For fixed s,j with h_s<j, put a=floor((j-h_s-1)/2), D=U-f+m.
The needed certificate for a given b is exactly the sufficient disjunction
 R_E(b+j)<=b+a OR R_R(D+j)<=b+a.
The whole-resource alternative covers all b>=R_R(D+j)-a. For smaller b,
put z=b+j and Z(z)=R_E(z)-z. Checking all b reduces to
 max_{j<=z<=min(j+bmax,j+R_R(D+j)-a-1)} Z(z)<=a-j,
with an empty range automatically passing.

Both range endpoints are nondecreasing in j: R_R(D+j) is nondecreasing,
and j-a is nondecreasing because a increases only every other step.
Therefore the monotone-deque range maximum in adaptive_density_probe.py
is correct. Finding one s that passes this entire b-range for each j is a
stronger sufficient quantifier than allowing s to depend on b, and is valid.

The supplied implementations use exact integer comparisons for both
rational densities. Histogram index d+1 and prefix sums represent strict
degree thresholds. Clamping negative degrees to -1 is harmless because all
queried thresholds are positive. Prime-list cutoff completeness is asserted.
The C++ certificate uses signed 128-bit intermediate products, with the
input restricted to U<=2,000,000. These bounds are far below overflow.

PART III Guarded finite prefix totient estimates
============================================================

MAIN RESULTS
Write G_n(t)=(2/n)*#{v<=n: v odd and phi(v)/v<t}; normalization is by n/2,
not ceil(n/2), so the bounds plug directly into the graph estimates.

For EVERY integer n>=1,000,000 and EVERY real t in [1/3,3/4],
 G_n(t) < (9/10)t-1/6-83/10000.                     (H)
This interval includes the needed endpoint 20/27 and guards it through .75.

For EVERY integer n>=2,000,000 and EVERY beta in [1/10,201/100],
 G_n(sqrt((beta+3/100)/3)) < beta/3-33/2500.         (L)
The remaining margin is .0132. In particular, before finite-prefix error,
the proposed .02 low-rank margin succeeds, with room to spare.

Both statements use only 16 states for the primes 3,5,7,11 and elementary
first-/sixth-moment estimates. No Mertens theorem or Rosser–Schoenfeld bound
is required. The exact checker uses <1 CPU-second and enumerates primes only
through 2000. It does not enumerate graph subsets A.

PROOF
1. Constants, all checked with exact fractions
Let lambda_p=-log(1-1/p), and let P={3,5,7,11}.
 delta_actual=sum_{p>11}lambda_p/p < D:=47/2000=.0235,
 C6=product_{odd p}(1+((p/(p-1))^6-1)/p)<11.
For each odd prime p<=2000, the first 12 terms of
lambda_p=sum_{h>=1}1/(h p^h) give a lower bound, with remainder at most
1/[13 p^12(p-1)]. Sum the resulting upper bounds for lambda_p/p and add
1/2000 for all omitted primes: lambda_p/p<=1/[p(p-1)], whose tail over
all integers p>2000 telescopes to 1/2000. Subtracting the four small-prime
lower bounds gives the displayed upper bound for delta_actual.
For C6, multiply its Euler factors through p<=200. For p>200 the binomial
expansion gives (p/(p-1))^6-1<=7/(p-1). Thus the omitted logarithmic tail
is <=7/200 and the product tail is <=exp(7/200)<=200/193.
Every finite sum and product is evaluated as an exact rational in the
bundled checker. Verified upper values:
 delta_actual<.023456772181318, C6<10.994755089901.

2. Uniform sixth moment, with no error term
For odd squarefree d, put c(d)=product_{p|d}((p/(p-1))^6-1), so
 (v/phi(v))^6=sum_{d|v}c(d), with nonnegative coefficients.
The number of odd multiples of d in [n] is at most n/d. Therefore
 (2/n) sum_{v<=n,v odd}(v/phi(v))^6 <=2 sum_d c(d)/d=2C6<22,
and Markov gives G_n(t)<22t^6, uniformly for EVERY n.

3. The 16-state bound and its finite-prefix discrepancy
For S subset P put
 a_S=product_{p in S}(1-1/p),
 q_S=product_{p in S}(1/p) product_{p in P\S}(1-1/p),
 e_S=2^(3-|S|).
Then sum q_S=1 and sum e_S=81/2.
For any odd d, its odd-multiple count K_d(n) is floor(n/(2d)+1/2), so
 |K_d(n)-n/(2d)|<=1/2.
By four-prime inclusion-exclusion, a signature count K_S satisfies
 |K_S-q_S*n/2|<=e_S.
For every prime p>11, the count K_{S,p} with that signature and p|v satisfies
 |K_{S,p}-q_S*n/(2p)|<=e_S.
Consequently, for T(v)=sum_{p>11,p|v}lambda_p,
 sum_{v<=n,v odd,signature S}T(v) <= q_S*(n/2)*D+e_S*L(n),
 L(n)=sum_{11<p<=n}lambda_p.
For n>=13 the elementary odd-integer comparison gives the sharp-enough bound
 L(n)<=sum_{j=6}^{floor((n-1)/2)}1/(2j)
      <=(1/2)log(floor((n-1)/2)/5)
      <=(1/2)log(n/10).                            (1)
No additive +.1 is necessary. Here lambda_p<=1/(p-1), and the harmonic-sum
bound follows by integrating 1/x from 5 to floor((n-1)/2).

If a_S<=t, use the signature count. Otherwise, Markov bounds that signature's
contribution to G_n(t) by
 min(q_S+2e_S/n,
     (q_S*D+2e_S*L(n)/n)/log(a_S/t)).
Use the rational lower bound r_S(t)=2(a_S-t)/(a_S+t)<=log(a_S/t), and set
 B_*(t)=sum_S q_S * u_S(t),
 u_S(t)=1                                    if a_S<=t,
 u_S(t)=min(1,D*(a_S+t)/(2(a_S-t)))            if a_S>t.
This function is nondecreasing.

For the error comparison, if r_S<D the limiting summand is q_S and the
signature-count error is <=2e_S/n. If r_S>=D, the extra Markov term is at
most 2e_S*L(n)/(D*n). For the n at issue L's upper bound can be taken >=D.
Summing proves
 G_n(t)<=B_*(t)+E(n),
 E(n)=(81/(2D))*log(n/10)/n
     =(81000/47)*log(n/10)/n.                       (2)
This comparison DOES NOT require an extra '+1' in E(n). Use the moment bound
with B(t)=min(22t^6,B_*(t)) to get G_n(t)<=B(t)+E(n).

4. Guarded high-branch certificate
Divide [1/3,3/4] into 1000 cells of width 1/2400. On every cell [l,r], the
exact-rational checker verifies
 (9/10)l-1/6-B(r)>29/1000.
The minimum actual cell slack is approximately .02905399178577378.
By monotonicity, B(t)<.9t-1/6-.029 everywhere on the interval.
For n>=10^6 the right side of (2) decreases with n. The exact rational check
sum_{k=0}^{20}12^k/k!>10^5 proves log(10^5)<12. Thus
 E(n)<=972000/47000000
 (diagnostic approximation: 972000/47000000 is approximately .02068085106382979),
 .029-E(n)>.0083,
which proves (H).

5. Low-rank endpoint certificate
Divide [1/10,201/100] into 1910 beta-cells [l,r] of width 1/1000. For each
right endpoint, bound sqrt((r+.03)/3) upward by a rational on the 10^-9 grid,
using integer isqrt and an exact squared comparison. The checker verifies
 l/3-B(upper_sqrt((r+.03)/3))>119/5000=.0238.
The minimum actual cell slack is approximately .02389295162749755.
By monotonicity, B(sqrt((beta+.03)/3))<beta/3-.0238 throughout the interval.
For n>=2*10^6, the exact check sum_{k=0}^{20}(49/4)^k/k!>2*10^5 proves
log(2*10^5)<49/4. Therefore
 E(n)<=3969000/376000000
 (diagnostic approximation: 3969000/376000000 is approximately .010555851063829787),
 .0238-E(n)>.0132,
which proves (L).

PART IV Explicit analytic proof above the threshold
============================================================

Let rho(v)=phi(v)/v, H=ceil(n/2),
 R_n(t)=#{odd v<=n:rho(v)<t}, G_n(t)=(2/n)R_n(t).
The supplied CDF certificate establishes exactly:
 (I) G_n(t)<22t^6 for every n and t>0;
 (II) G_n(t)<0.9t-1/6-0.0083 for n>=1,000,000,
      1/3<=t<=3/4;
 (III) G_n(sqrt((beta+0.03)/3))<beta/3-0.0132 for
       n>=2,000,000 and 0.1<=beta<=2.01.
Input (I) follows from C6<11, expansion into nonnegative divisor weights,
and the upper bound n/d for the number of odd multiples of odd d. In
particular it has no additive finite-n error. Inputs (II),(III) already
include every finite-prefix error; none should be added again below.

For every n>1024 choose the least s with n<=4^s and use the first s odd
prime divisibility coordinates. The adaptive-tail certificate establishes
that a same-signature pair with both profiles >=t has common-neighbor
density >=0.9t. Every pair, without a signature condition, has density
>=t^2. Inclusion-exclusion gives common-neighbor loss <(3/2)sqrt(n).
Moving the two coprime skeleton endpoints to the ends leaves at most
h=2^s+1<2sqrt(n)+1 cross-signature gaps. These are the only graph-arithmetic
lemmas used below.

1. Exact integer accounting and uniform numerical bounds
Fix n>=N=2,000,000. Write n=6m+r, 0<=r<=5. Let
 q=floor(n/2), f=floor(n/2)+floor(n/3)-m,
 M=f-q+1, b=#missing even integers,
 beta=b/m, x=j/m for 1<=j<=m,
 D=n-f+m=3m+(0,1,1,1,1,2)_r.
Feasibility gives 0<=b<=H-M and beta<=2+1/m.
Also q>=3m, H<=3m+3,
 n/(2m)<3.000008, and 22H/m<66.001.

Let delta=(3/2)sqrt(n), and epsilon=(delta+2)/m. The proven discrepancy
bound for every odd endpoint pair is strictly below delta. Choose the
least s with n<=4^s; its transition budget h=2^s+1 satisfies h<2sqrt(n)+1,
and the adaptive tail factor is at least 9/10.
Uniformly throughout n>=N,
 epsilon<0.0065, h/m<0.0086.                         (1)
To check these without relying on unrecorded decimals, m>=(n-5)/6, and the
upper functions (9sqrt(n)+12)/(n-5), (12sqrt(n)+6)/(n-5) decrease for n>5.
At N use sqrt(N)<1415; cross multiplication gives both bounds in (1).

For t<=1, let G_n(t)=(2/n)R_n(t). The integer 1 is never counted in
R_n(t), so 0<=G_n(t)<=1. Since n/(2m)<3.000008,
 R_n(t)/m < 3G_n(t)+0.000008.                       (2)
For the direct moment estimate we may use the slightly weaker convenient
bound R_n(t)/m<66.001t^6.

2. Why the chosen thresholds supply sufficient resources
For a shared-s-signature pair with both endpoint profiles >=t, the common
neighbor counts are at least
 even pool: q(9/10)t-delta,
 whole pool: n(9/10)t-delta.
For an arbitrary pair, with no signature requirement, they are at least
 q t^2-delta and n t^2-delta, respectively.

For every rank j the even-pool target is b+j; the whole-pool target is D+j.
These account for all missing evens, or all missing vertices and the entire
odd skeleton, respectively.

Retain the best M selected odds. At most max(0,R_n(t)-b) retained odds have
profile <t. In a skeleton ordered by the full s-signature and with the two
coprime endpoints moved to its ends, at most
 2 max(0,R_n(t)-b)+h
successive gaps are exceptional. If the last expression is <j, and one of
the common-neighbor targets holds, the rank-j Hall condition follows.
For an unstructured, product-density bound one uses h=0, with the same
fixed skeleton order. Hence the following rankwise cases are compatible.

3. Large ranks: x>=0.02
Choose
 t=min((beta+x+epsilon)/2.7, (3+x+epsilon)/5.4).       (4)
This t is positive and
 t<4.0065/5.4<0.743.
If the first branch in (4) is minimal, the even lower bound is at least
 2.7m t-delta=b+j+2.
If the second is minimal, the whole lower bound is at least
 5.4m t-delta=3m+j+2>=D+j.
Thus a required resource target always holds.

3a. The high-profile branch t>=10/27
The finite CDF lemma gives
 G_n(t)<0.9t-1/6-0.0083
because 1/3<=t<0.743<3/4 and n>=2,000,000.
Using (2) and t<=(beta+x+epsilon)/2.7,
 R_n(t)/m
 <2.7t-1/2-0.0249+0.000008
 <=beta+x+epsilon-0.5249+0.000008
 <=beta+x/2-0.0249+epsilon+0.000008,
since x<=1. Moreover
 epsilon+h/(2m)+0.000008
 <0.0065+0.0043+0.000008
 =0.010808<0.0249.
As x>h/m, this proves
 2 max(0,R_n(t)/m-beta)+h/m<x.
The rank-j condition follows with strict inequality.

3b. The low-profile branch t<10/27
The second branch of (4) is >5/9, so the first branch must be minimal.
Set y=beta+x+epsilon=2.7t; then 0.02<=y<1.
From the direct finite moment bound and 22H/m<66.001,
 R_n(t)/m <66.001(y/2.7)^6<0.171 y^6.               (5)
The polynomial F(y)=0.342y^6-y+0.0151 is convex on [0.02,1], and both
endpoint values are strictly negative. Thus
 0.342y^6+0.0086<y-0.0065<=y-epsilon=beta+x<=2beta+x.
Together with x>=0.02>h/m, this and (5) imply the same strict rank-j
condition as in 3a. The direct finite moment bound here has no additive error term.

4. Small ranks with many missing evens: x<=0.02, beta>=0.1
Set beta_0=min(beta,2) and
 t=sqrt((beta_0+0.03)/3).
The clipping handles n=6m+5, when b can equal 2m+1; then
 0<=m(beta-beta_0)<=1.
Using the unstructured product bound, the common even-neighbor count is
at least
 q t^2-delta>=m(beta_0+0.03)-delta.
This exceeds b+j: the difference is at least
 0.01m-delta-1 > 0.0035m+1 >0,
using epsilon=(delta+2)/m<0.0065.

By the direct low-rank finite CDF lemma and (2),
 R_n(t)/m < beta_0-3(0.0132)+0.000008
 =beta_0-0.039592<beta.
Therefore R_n(t)<b. Every retained odd has profile >=t; no gap is
exceptional and every connector list has at least j members. No signature
transition budget is needed in this case.

5. Universal common-even-neighbor bound
For every odd pair u,v<=n, write w=omega(rad(uv)). Since its distinct odd
prime factors are at least 3,5,...,2w+1,
 rho(rad(uv))>=product_{i=1}^w 2i/(2i+1)
              >=sqrt(3/(4w+3)).                    (6)
The second inequality follows by induction: at step w,
 [2w/(2w+1)]^2 >= (4w-1)/(4w+3),
whose cross-multiplied numerators differ by exactly 1. Also
 w<=2log(n)/log(3),
so the common even-neighbor count is at least
 ((n-1)/2)sqrt(3/(8log(n)/log(3)+3))-(3/2)sqrt(n).
This quantity is >30sqrt(n) for every n>=N.

For completeness, divide the first term by sqrt(n) and call it G(n).
Its square is a positive constant times
 (n-1)^2/[n(8log(n)/log(3)+3)],
which is increasing for n>=3: the logarithmic derivative comparison is
 (n+1)/(n-1)>[8/log(3)]/[8log(n)/log(3)+3].
At N, log(N)<15, log(3)>1, and sqrt(N)<1500. Thus the square-root factor
in G(N) is >1/7, and
 G(N)>(N-1)/(14sqrt(N))>(N-1)/21000>31.5.
Subtracting 1.5 proves the claim. The elementary logarithm inequalities
follow from e>8/3 and e<3. This intentionally conservative bound is enough.

6. Small ranks with few missing evens: x<=0.02, beta<0.1
If b+j<=30sqrt(n), Section 5 shows that every odd pair has at least b+j
common evens. Removing at most b missing evens leaves at least j resources
for every gap, so the rank-j condition is automatic.

Otherwise b+j>30sqrt(n), hence delta<(b+j)/20. Put z=beta+x<0.12 and
 t=sqrt(1.05z/3).
The unstructured common-even-neighbor bound is strictly greater than
 3m t^2-delta=1.05(b+j)-delta>b+j.
The direct finite moment bound gives
 R_n(t)/m<66.001(1.05z/3)^3<2.84z^3.                (7)
If beta>=x, then beta>=z/2 and
 2.84z^3<z/2<=beta,
since 5.68(0.12)^2<1. Thus no retained low-profile vertices remain.
If beta<x, then z<2x and (7) gives
 2 max(0,R_n(t)/m-beta)<=2R_n(t)/m<45.44x^3<x,
since x<=0.02 and 45.44(0.02)^2<1.
In both cases fewer than j gaps have insufficient common-neighbor bounds.
There is no signature-transition term because the product-density estimate
is unstructured.

7. Conclusion and exact dependency boundary
Sections 3,4,6 cover every feasible b and every rank 1<=j<=m. The bounds
hold for one fixed skeleton order: the adaptive full signature in Section 3,
with the same ordering harmless in the unstructured cases. The two prescribed
odd endpoints are coprime by the exact Ahlswede--Khachatrian theorem.
Ordered Hall supplies every required odd cycle for every n>=2,000,000.

PART V Exact finite bridge and completion
============================================================

For n<=5, the theorem has no target odd cycle length at least 3 and is
vacuous. The supplied interval list interval_cover_6_2300.json contains
71 adjacent intervals covering every integer from 6 to 2300. Each interval
passes the exact criterion in Part I using the first four odd primes.
The script check_interval_cover.py generates the cover. Independent
verification recomputes the common-neighbor profiles by direct gcd counts;
a separate C++ verifier recomputes them by exact inclusion–exclusion.

The file adaptive_bridge_2301_2000000_output.txt contains 84 adjacent PASS
intervals covering every integer from 2301 to 2,000,000. The integer-only
program adaptive_bridge.cpp verifies the profile-only form of the same
interval criterion, using g_s(z)=max(z^2,eta_s(U)z) from Part II. It checks
all Hall ranks and all feasible missing-even counts through exact range
maxima. No selected subset A is sampled or enumerated.

A separately rebuilt source passes the same 84 intervals. A further
independently written verifier uses a linear smallest-prime-factor totient
sieve and segment-tree range maxima rather than the original sieve and
monotone deque. It also passes all 84 intervals. The separate mathematical
audit and checker sources are included in the bundle.

REJECT lines in diagnostic logs refer to wider proposed intervals that
failed a sufficient test and were split. Every interval in the final
contiguous covers passes. Such rejected proposals are not counterexamples
to the theorem and do not leave gaps in the cover.

Combining these exact covers with Part IV proves the stated all-n theorem
within this proof candidate. The overlap at n=2,000,000 is deliberate.
No extrapolation from finite samples, no unspecified n0 and no unverified
large finite range is used in this combination.
